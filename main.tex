\begin{abstract}
In emergency mobile tele-radiology, brain MRI scans must be heavily undersampled to stream over cellular networks within the critical stroke ``golden hour''. Hospital edge servers reconstruct the missing data, while conventional Quality of Service (QoS) metrics verify transmission quality. In this paper, we expose the ``QoS Illusion'' in medical streaming: reconstruction pipelines optimized for global pixel fidelity can silently erase small acute strokes into smooth, normal-looking tissue. Because standard network metrics average errors over millions of pixels, and a missing stroke changes the received measurements by less than scanner noise, these omissions pass edge network checks undetected while the network dashboard reports flawless transmission. We first formalize this dual mechanism analytically. We then introduce the Causal Safety Audit (CSA), a task-based QoS audit that measures actual lesion detectability across the streaming pipeline, and a four-layer diagnostic-aware transmission architecture built on it. Across 3,960 counterfactual test pairs constructed from clinical multi-coil brain MRI, aggressive undersampling silently erases acute strokes in up to nearly 30\% of cases despite pristine QoS scores, and diagnostic-aware rate allocation is projected to reduce this below 1\% at the same payload. Conventional rate-distortion metrics therefore cannot safeguard diagnostic integrity, motivating task-aware, diagnostic-safe medical streaming protocols.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In emergency neurology, ``time is brain'': an untreated acute ischemic stroke destroys roughly two million neurons per minute \cite{saver2006time}. Mobile Stroke Units (MSUs) bring imaging into the ambulance and shorten the time to thrombolysis \cite{fassbender2017mobile}, and ultrafast two-minute brain MRI protocols with deep learning (DL) reconstruction are now clinically validated for suspected stroke \cite{altmann2024ultrafast,lang2024twominute}. The definitive read, however, still requires a hospital neuroradiologist, so the scan must be streamed from the moving vehicle over a cellular (4G/5G) uplink \cite{ahad2019healthcare,mahmeen2021radiology}.

\textit{Communication bottleneck.} A multi-coil brain MRI acquisition consists of spatial-frequency ($k$-space) data from 12--32 receiver coils and routinely exceeds 200--500\,MB \cite{zbontar2018fastmri}. At the tens of Mbps available to a moving ambulance, raw transmission takes minutes and violates stroke triage deadlines. Post-acquisition compression (JPEG-2000, H.265) does not help: it neither shortens the physical scan nor removes the need for onboard reconstruction hardware that ambulances lack \cite{wang2020convergence}. The practical solution is to acquire and transmit only a fraction $1/R$ of $k$-space at \emph{acceleration factor} $R$, which shortens the scan and cuts the payload by $1-1/R$ ($75\%$ to $87.5\%$ for $R=4$ to $8$), and to reconstruct the missing frequencies at a hospital edge server using parallel imaging \cite{pruessmann1999sense} or DL models such as End-to-End Variational Networks \cite{sriram2020endtoend}. Throughout, $R$ denotes this acceleration factor; because it fixes the transmitted fraction of data, no separate compression factor is needed.

\textit{Problem statement.} Network rate-distortion optimization (RDO) and QoS monitoring judge such a stream by global distortion metrics---Mean Squared Error (MSE), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity (SSIM) \cite{wang2004image}---and by data-consistency checks on the received measurements. Neither asks whether a specific small lesion survived. Reconstruction models trained on pixel-averaged losses have a documented incentive to replace small high-contrast features with smooth background \cite{antun2020instabilities,bhadra2021hallucinations}, and a systematic review of 263 studies found that over $71\%$ of DL reconstruction papers evaluate only such pixel averages \cite{mai2026metric}. Because an acute lacunar stroke occupies a tiny fraction of the brain (often $<50$ voxels), its disappearance is numerically negligible to every indicator the network sees. We call this gap the \textbf{QoS Illusion}: the network certifies the transmission as healthy while the diagnosis it carried has been lost.

\textit{Contributions.} This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formalize the dual mechanism of the QoS Illusion in two propositions: an image-domain spatial-averaging bound showing why erasing a focal lesion barely moves PSNR or SSIM, and a measurement-domain null-space result showing why data-consistency gates cannot detect it.
    \item We propose the Causal Safety Audit (CSA), a task-based QoS audit that inserts physically exact counterfactual lesions into raw $k$-space before transmission and measures their detectability after reconstruction with a Channelised Hotelling Observer (CHO), defining \emph{silent erasure} as a measurable network failure mode.
    \item We propose a four-layer diagnostic-aware transmission architecture: CSA auditing, Diagnostic-Aware Rate-Distortion Optimization (DARDO) of the sampling budget, 5G URLLC slicing of diagnostically critical bands, and semantic tokens with selective retransmission.
    \item We evaluate the audit on $3{,}960$ counterfactual pairs from the NYU fastMRI multi-coil brain dataset, quantifying how silent erasure grows with $R$ while every conventional QoS indicator stays stable, and project the protection that DARDO affords at the same payload.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:theory} presents the system model and the two propositions. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes CSA and the proposed architecture. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the experimental results, the end-to-end latency budget and the limitations of the study, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{System Model and the Dual Mechanism of the QoS Illusion}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{System Model}
We model the acquisition and transmission pipeline as a linear inverse problem. Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{C}^N$ denote the true cross-sectional patient image. The MSU scanner acquires the multi-coil $k$-space measurement $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{C}^M$, with $M = N/R \ll N$, governed by
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{X} + \boldsymbol{\eta} = \mathcal{M} \mathcal{F} \mathcal{S} \mathbf{X} + \boldsymbol{\eta},
\label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ represents the receiver coil sensitivity profiles, $\mathcal{F}$ is the 2D spatial Fourier transform, $\mathcal{M}$ is the subsampling mask that retains $M$ phase-encoding lines at acceleration factor $R$, and $\boldsymbol{\eta} \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, \sigma_\eta^2 \mathbf{I})$ is zero-mean complex Gaussian thermal noise. The $M$ acquired samples are streamed over the cellular uplink to the hospital edge server, which reconstructs $\hat{\mathbf{X}}$ and reports two QoS indicators: a global fidelity score (PSNR/SSIM against a reference in benchmarking, or a no-reference proxy in deployment) and the data-consistency residual $\|\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{Y}\|_2$. Only if both pass is $\hat{\mathbf{X}}$ released to the radiologist. The question we ask is whether either indicator responds when a focal lesion $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ present in $\mathbf{X}$ is absent from $\hat{\mathbf{X}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:dual_blindspot}).

\subsection{The Image-Domain Spatial Averaging Mechanism}
Network RDO evaluates the reconstruction via $\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N}\|\hat{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{X}\|_2^2$ and $\text{PSNR} = 10\log_{10}(\text{MAX}^2/\text{MSE})$.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Metric Shift Under Focal Pathology):} Let $\boldsymbol{\delta} \in \mathbb{C}^N$ denote a localized lesion perturbation occupying volume fraction $f = K/N \ll 1$, where $K$ is the lesion pixel count. Suppose background pixels incur average squared error $\epsilon_{\text{bg}}$, while the lesion region incurs average error $\epsilon_{\text{lesion}} = r \epsilon_{\text{bg}}$, with $r > 1$ representing severe local omission. The resulting global PSNR shift satisfies
\begin{equation}
|\Delta\text{PSNR}| = 10\log_{10}\left(1 + f(r - 1)\right).
\label{eq:psnr_bound}
\end{equation}
\textit{Derivation:} Because MSE is a pixel average, the corrupted error is $\text{MSE}_{\text{pert}} = (1-f)\epsilon_{\text{bg}} + fr\epsilon_{\text{bg}} = \epsilon_{\text{bg}}\big(1 + f(r-1)\big)$. Substituting $\text{MSE}_{\text{pert}}$ and the baseline $\text{MSE}_{\text{clean}} = \epsilon_{\text{bg}}$ into the PSNR definition yields (\ref{eq:psnr_bound}). SSIM behaves identically: it is the spatial mean of local structure scores over all windows, so a lesion covering $K$ of $N$ pixels shifts it by $\mathcal{O}(K/N)$.

\textit{Physical Intuition:} For a standard $320 \times 320 \times 16$ multi-slice brain volume ($N \approx 1.64 \times 10^6$ voxels at $0.7 \times 0.7 \times 5\,\text{mm}^3$), an acute $100\,\text{mm}^3$ lacunar infarct occupies $K \approx 41$ voxels, giving $f \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-5}$. Even under complete erasure with a hundredfold local error ($r = 100$), the global shift is
\begin{equation*}
|\Delta\text{PSNR}| = 10\log_{10}(1 + 2.5\times 10^{-5} \times 99) \approx 0.0107\,\text{dB}.
\end{equation*}
Diluted across 1.64 million background voxels, wiping out the stroke produces an imperceptible $0.01\,\text{dB}$ shift, invisible to telemetry that reports PSNR to one or two decimals (Fig.~\ref{fig:dual_blindspot}b).

\subsection{The Measurement-Domain Null-Space Invariance}
To verify integrity without ground truth, network protocols enforce physical data consistency, $\|\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{Y}\|_2 \le \tau_{\text{res}}$.

\textbf{Proposition 2 (Residual Insensitivity to Null-Space Perturbations):} Let $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^*$ be the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the multi-coil forward operator, with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_M > 0$. Decompose any lesion perturbation into range-space and null-space components, $\boldsymbol{\delta} = \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathcal{R}} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathcal{N}}$, with $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathcal{N}} \in \text{null}(\mathbf{A})$. The change in the raw measurement residual satisfies
\begin{equation}
\big| \|\mathbf{A}(\hat{\mathbf{X}} + \boldsymbol{\delta}) - \mathbf{Y}\|_2 - \|\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{Y}\|_2 \big| \le \|\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathcal{R}}\|_2 .
\label{eq:null_bound}
\end{equation}
For purely unacquired frequencies $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathcal{N}}$, the residual change is identically zero.

\textit{Physical Intuition:} A receiver cannot verify frequency bands that were never transmitted. With non-ideal coil sensitivities and thermal noise $\sigma_\eta$, the effective numerical null space contains all components with $\sigma_i < \sigma_\eta$. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dual_blindspot}c, perturbations in this subspace alter the residual by less than the noise floor, so diagnostic omissions pass edge consistency checks undetected.

\section{Proposed Causal Safety Audit and Diagnostic-Aware Architecture}
\label{sec:method}

Rather than relying on aggregate pixel distortion, the Causal Safety Audit (CSA) stress-tests a transmission pipeline with exact physical counterfactuals and a task-based observer, and anchors a four-layer architecture that allocates, protects and verifies the diagnostically critical part of the stream.

\subsection{Physical Counterfactual Lesion Insertion}
Ground truth is unavailable at edge decoders, but because MRI physics is linear we can construct exact counterfactuals by superimposing test pathologies into raw $k$-space \emph{before} transmission:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Y}_{\text{cf}} = \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{X} + \boldsymbol{\delta}) + \boldsymbol{\eta} = \mathbf{Y} + \mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta},
\label{eq:linear_cf}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ is an acute focal lesion of volume $V \in [10, 200]\,\text{mm}^3$ and contrast $C$ inserted at verified anatomical sites. Pre-transmission superimposition ensures that $\mathbf{Y}$ and $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{cf}}$ share identical anatomy $\mathbf{X}$, coil sensitivities $\mathcal{S}$, mask $\mathcal{M}$ and thermal noise $\boldsymbol{\eta}$, without generative hallucinations.

\subsection{Task-Based Detectability via Model Observers}
Automated tele-radiology QoS requires an objective detection metric rather than cosmetic pixel fidelity. Lesion detection under anatomical clutter is a binary hypothesis test in colored noise, for which we deploy the Channelised Hotelling Observer (CHO) \cite{barrett1993model}, an optimal \emph{pre-whitening matched filter} across four spatial Gabor sub-bands. Let $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ denote the filter responses at the target coordinates, and $\Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}} = \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{present}} - \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{absent}}$ the mean signal shift between lesion-present and lesion-absent reconstructions. The Hotelling template $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{K}^{-1}\Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}$ whitens the intra-channel background covariance $\mathbf{K}$, and the detectability index $d'$ is the matched-filter SNR:
\begin{equation}
d' = \frac{\mathbf{w}^T \Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{\sqrt{\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{w}}} = \sqrt{\Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}^T \mathbf{K}^{-1} \Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}} .
\label{eq:dprime}
\end{equation}
Standardizing $d'$ gives a per-lesion detectability score $z$ on the fully sampled reference ($z_{\text{ref}}$) and on the transmitted reconstruction ($z_{\text{recon}}$). The CHO is an established surrogate for human visual detectability \cite{barrett1993model}; multi-reader studies remain the complementary clinical validation.

\subsection{Silent Erasure Definition}
We define \textbf{Silent Erasure} (SE) as the failure mode in which transmission destroys clinical detectability while reporting normal network QoS:
\begin{equation}
\text{SE} \iff z_{\text{ref}} \ge z_{\text{det}} \,\wedge\, z_{\text{recon}} < z_{\text{miss}} \,\wedge\, \text{PSNR} \in \mathcal{I}_{95},
\label{eq:silent_erasure}
\end{equation}
where $z_{\text{det}} = 3.0$ (lesion clearly visible in the fully sampled reference) and $z_{\text{miss}} = 2.0$ (lesion missed after transmission) were fixed before any reconstruction was run, pairs with $2.0 \le z_{\text{recon}} < 3.0$ are indeterminate, and $\mathcal{I}_{95}$ is the central $95\%$ interval of global PSNR over lesion-free reconstructions at the same $R$. The dashboard reports normal throughput and high fidelity, but the patient's acute stroke has vanished.

\subsection{Four-Layer Diagnostic-Aware Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
Resolving the QoS Illusion requires more than a better metric. We propose a four-layer diagnostic-aware architecture spanning perception, source coding, radio access and transport (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}): \textbf{Layer~1 (Perception \& Audit)} embeds CSA into transmission pre-checks via the task-based CHO; \textbf{Layer~2 (Diagnostic Compression)} introduces DARDO, which allocates the sampling budget in proportion to diagnostic spectral density; \textbf{Layer~3 (5G RAN Slicing)} routes mission-critical stroke frequencies over 5G URLLC slices while background anatomy travels over eMBB; and \textbf{Layer~4 (Semantic Transport)} carries 128-byte semantic tokens in QUIC headers, enabling targeted S-NACK retransmission of missing stroke frequencies.

\subsection{Diagnostic-Aware Rate-Distortion Optimization (DARDO)}
Conventional RDO minimizes the mean squared pixel error $\min_{\mathcal{M}} \|\hat{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{X}\|_2^2$, spending the bit budget on high-energy low frequencies that define gross skull shape while discarding subtle high-frequency stroke signatures. DARDO re-formulates source coding as task-constrained rate minimization:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathcal{M}} \quad \text{Rate}(\mathcal{M}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad d'_{\text{CHO}}(\mathcal{M}) \ge d'_{\text{threshold}},
\label{eq:dardo}
\end{equation}
where $d'_{\text{threshold}}$ corresponds to the miss threshold $z_{\text{miss}}$ of (\ref{eq:silent_erasure}). To solve (\ref{eq:dardo}) in real time on mobile edge hardware without combinatorial search, we specify a greedy allocation heuristic: (1)~\textbf{Task Spectral Profiling:} pre-compute the task spectral power density $W(k_x, k_y) = |\mathcal{F}\{\mathbf{w}\}|^2$ of the CHO matched filter; (2)~\textbf{Channel Utility Scoring:} rank candidate phase-encode lines by diagnostic gradient density,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_k = \sum_{y} W(k, y) \|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_k\|_2 ,
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_k$ denotes the coil sensitivities; and (3)~\textbf{Greedy Line Allocation:} seed $\mathcal{M}$ with the autocalibration centre lines and iteratively insert the lines maximizing $\gamma_k$ until $d'_{\text{CHO}}(\mathcal{M}) \ge d'_{\text{threshold}}$ or the budget $M = N/R$ is exhausted. Because $W$ is pre-calculated offline, line sorting runs in $\mathcal{O}(M\log M)$ time ($<2\,\text{ms}$ on mobile edge CPUs). Under extreme payload reduction ($R=8$) the finite budget cannot protect every lesion: DARDO prioritizes decisive acute strokes, while any residual loss of sub-clinical micro-infarcts is handled by Layer~4 retransmission.

\subsection{5G URLLC Slicing and Prioritized Scheduling}
MSUs face unpredictable channel fading, shadowing and handovers. Under uniform best-effort streaming (e.g., DASH), dropped packet bursts in high-frequency stroke bands cause the edge AI to reconstruct smooth, false-normal tissue. In Layer~3 the transmitter tags packets by diagnostic utility: (1)~\textbf{Critical Slice (5G URLLC):} packets carrying high-utility lines ($\gamma_k \ge \tau$) are mapped to a delay-critical guaranteed-bit-rate 5QI class (5QI 82--85 \cite{3gpp23501}), for which the radio scheduler targets a block error rate (BLER) $<10^{-5}$ and sub-10\,ms latency under 3GPP Rel-16 URLLC; and (2)~\textbf{Bulk Slice (5G eMBB):} lines of low diagnostic density ($\gamma_k < \tau$) stream over best-effort eMBB, absorbing transient congestion without risking diagnostic integrity.

\subsection{Task-Based Semantic Tokens and Closed-Loop S-NACK}
In 6G semantic communications \cite{zhang2022semantic}, decoders verify diagnostic meaning rather than syntactic bit identity. To audit fidelity in blind clinical settings without ground-truth labels, the transmitter embeds a 128-byte semantic probe in the QUIC/RTP header, containing pre-computed Gabor responses $\mathbf{v}$ and suspicious vascular coordinates from pre-hospital scout scans. If the edge reconstruction yields $z_{\text{recon}} < z_{\text{miss}}$, a targeted \textbf{Selective Negative Acknowledgment (S-NACK)} is issued over the URLLC slice. Rather than retransmitting the full volume ($>200\,$MB), S-NACK requests only the missing high-$\gamma_k$ bands ($\Delta k \approx 1.2\,\text{MB}$), restoring detectability in $\approx 15\,\text{ms}$.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Dataset and Experimental Setup}
We evaluate the audit on the NYU fastMRI multi-coil brain benchmark \cite{zbontar2018fastmri} (clinical 3T T1 and FLAIR acquisitions with 16- and 20-channel head arrays). The test set comprises $3{,}960$ counterfactual evaluation pairs generated from 22 independent patient scans (11 AXFLAIR, 11 AXT1) over 7 slice positions ($N=264$ balanced pairs per volume--acceleration cell), spanning: (1)~\textbf{Acceleration factor:} $R \in \{4, 6, 8\}$, transmitting $25\%$, $16.7\%$ and $12.5\%$ of $k$-space; (2)~\textbf{Lesion volume:} $V \in \{10, 27, 64, 100, 200\}\,\text{mm}^3$, from micro-infarcts ($10\,\text{mm}^3 \approx 2.7\,\text{mm}$ diameter) to acute lacunar strokes ($64\,\text{mm}^3 \approx 5.0\,\text{mm}$) and confluent lesions ($200\,\text{mm}^3$); (3)~\textbf{Contrast:} $C \in \{0.24, 1.0, 1.5\}$ (FLAIR) and $\{-0.21, -0.6, -1.0\}$ (T1); and (4)~\textbf{Reconstruction:} zero-filled inverse Fourier reconstruction and CG-SENSE parallel imaging \cite{pruessmann1999sense} ($1{,}980$ pairs each), with a learned-prior reconstruction examined qualitatively in Section~\ref{sec:case_study}. Code and data: \url{https://github.com/dmai287/tele-radiology-blindspot}.

\subsection{Rate-Distortion vs. Silent Erasure Trade-Off}
Table~\ref{tab:rate_distortion} and Fig.~\ref{fig:network_rd} show the divergence between network-level QoS and diagnostic safety. Under conventional RDO (Fig.~\ref{fig:network_rd}a), every QoS indicator behaves exactly as a network operator expects: raising $R$ from 4 to 8 lowers global PSNR from $29.4$ to $25.3\,$dB and SSIM from $0.846$ to $0.760$---a smooth, monotonic rate-distortion curve with no anomaly. The indicator that would have to flag the hazard, the lesion-induced shift, stays flat and negligible: $|\Delta\text{PSNR}| \le 0.016\,$dB and $|\Delta\text{SSIM}| \le 2\times10^{-4}$ at every $R$, two orders of magnitude below the resolution of telemetry and consistent with Proposition~1. Network monitors therefore certify the stream as healthy at all three rates.

Diagnostic integrity, in contrast, collapses (Fig.~\ref{fig:network_rd}b--c). For $64\,\text{mm}^3$ acute lacunar strokes ($N=264$ per cell), silent erasure rises from $18.2\%$ ($R=4$) to $\mathbf{28.8\%}$ ($R=8$; $95\%$ CI $[23.3\%, 34.2\%]$), i.e., nearly one in three strokes is lost, while mean detectability for this lesion falls from $z=1.74$ to $1.05$. Averaged over all volumes, erasure grows from $12.7\%$ to $17.4\%$ and mean $z_{\text{recon}}$ falls from $1.47$ to $1.12$.

\subsection{Projected Protection Under DARDO}
\label{sec:dardo_results}
To estimate the benefit of DARDO on the same $3{,}960$ pairs without re-acquiring data, we use a first-order spectral-energy model: for each (volume, $R$) cell we compute the fraction of task spectral energy $\gamma_k$ captured by the DARDO mask of Section~\ref{sec:method}-E versus the equispaced mask at the same budget $M = N/R$, and rescale each pair's measured detectability loss $z_{\text{ref}} - z_{\text{recon}}$ by the fraction of task energy DARDO recovers. This assumes detectability loss is proportional to unrecovered task energy; it is an optimistic estimate that a full DARDO reconstruction must confirm (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Under this model (Tables~\ref{tab:rate_distortion} and \ref{tab:comparison}): (1)~\textbf{Acute stroke:} no $64\,\text{mm}^3$ stroke is erased at any $R$ (0 of 264 pairs per cell, against $28.8\%$ conventionally at $R=8$) at identical $87.5\%$ payload savings; (2)~\textbf{All volumes:} mean erasure at $R=8$ falls from $17.4\%$ to $\mathbf{0.83\%}$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.34\%, 1.32\%]$) and mean $z_{\text{recon}}$ rises from $1.12$ to $2.19$, above $z_{\text{miss}}$; and (3)~\textbf{Residual loss:} the only cell DARDO does not clear is $10\,\text{mm}^3$ micro-infarcts at $R=8$ ($11.7\% \to 4.17\%$), the residual that Layer~4 S-NACK retransmission is designed to absorb.

\subsection{Physical Residual Invariance Verification}
To confirm Proposition~2, Table~\ref{tab:nullspace} evaluates measurement residuals across clinical brain slices at $R=8$. Inserting focal lesions produced relative image edits of up to $1.1\%$ ($\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_2 / \|\mathbf{X}\|_2 = 0.0108$), yet the raw residual changed by only $10^{-9}$--$10^{-8}$. With scanner thermal noise $\sigma_\eta \sim 10^{-2}$, these shifts sit six orders of magnitude below the noise floor: edge verification gates cannot distinguish a pristine scan from an erased stroke because the missing features lie entirely in the null space.

\subsection{End-to-End Latency Budget}
Within the stroke ``golden hour'', we target a scan-to-decision time of $<105\,$s. Clinically validated ultrafast protocols acquire a five-sequence brain examination in about two minutes \cite{lang2024twominute,altmann2024ultrafast}; we budget $90\,$s for the stroke-relevant subset. At $R=8$ ($37.5\,$MB over an assumed $30\,\text{Mbps}$ 5G uplink) the budget is: (1)~ultrafast scan, $90.0\,\text{s}$; (2)~DARDO line sorting, $1.8\,\text{ms}$; (3)~5G uplink, $10.0\,\text{s}$; (4)~VarNet GPU reconstruction \cite{sriram2020endtoend}, $1.8\,\text{s}$; (5)~CHO audit, $12.0\,\text{ms}$; and (6)~worst-case S-NACK, $15.0\,\text{ms}$. The total of $\mathbf{101.8\,\text{s}} < 105\,\text{s}$ shows that task-based verification fits inside the emergency deadline. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes the resulting protocol contrast.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
Four limitations bound our claims. (1)~\emph{Simulated lesions:} counterfactual insertion is physically exact in $k$-space, but the inserted lesions are spherical with uniform contrast, whereas real infarcts are heterogeneous; validation on annotated clinical stroke cohorts is required. (2)~\emph{Model observer:} a multi-reader study must confirm that $z_{\text{det}}$ and $z_{\text{miss}}$ track radiologist performance. (3)~\emph{Reconstruction scope:} the quantitative audit covers zero-filled and CG-SENSE reconstructions, while learned-prior erasure (Fig.~\ref{fig:case_study}) is shown qualitatively; a full audit of DL reconstructions is in progress. (4)~\emph{Network-level validation:} the DARDO figures are first-order projections, and Layers~3--4 are specified and budgeted analytically against 3GPP targets rather than measured on a live network; packet-level emulation under fading, handover and congestion, and a DARDO reconstruction run, are needed before the latency and reliability claims can be considered validated.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We exposed the QoS Illusion in tele-radiology: standard rate-distortion metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and data-consistency gates cannot detect catastrophic diagnostic omissions under network payload reduction. Analytically, erasing an acute $100\,\text{mm}^3$ stroke shifts PSNR by $<0.03\,\text{dB}$ and the measurement residual by $<10^{-8}$, both lost in noise. Across $3{,}960$ fastMRI counterfactual pairs, raising $R$ from $4$ to $8$ drove silent stroke erasure from $18.2\%$ to $28.8\%$ while every conventional QoS indicator declined smoothly and the lesion-induced shift stayed below $0.02\,$dB. We proposed the Causal Safety Audit and a four-layer diagnostic-aware architecture (task observers, DARDO, 5G URLLC slicing, closed-loop semantic tokens); a first-order projection indicates that DARDO can prevent acute stroke erasure at identical $87.5\%$ payload savings within a $101.8\,$s triage budget. Validating this projection with full reconstructions, reader studies and live-network emulation is ongoing work.
